# Headline Writing Guide

Headlines are single sentences that state the insight, not just the topic. They advance the story by telling the audience what to think, not just what to look at.

## Why Headlines Matter

A title labels. A headline argues. In business storytelling, every section should be making a point — if it isn't, it probably shouldn't exist. Headlines force that discipline.

If you can't write a headline for a section, ask: what is this section actually claiming? If there's no answer, cut the section or merge it with one that does have a point.

---

## The Headline Formula

**[Subject] + [Action/Change] + [So-What / Implication]**

Not every headline needs all three, but the best ones usually have a subject, something that is happening or has changed, and a reason why the audience should care.

## Before / After Examples

### Status Updates

| Title (weak) | Headline (strong) |
|---|---|
| Q3 Results | Q3 revenue exceeded target by 12%, driven by two channels — now we need to double down before Q4 budget is locked |
| Project Update | Phase 2 is on track but our key vendor dependency is a risk — here's our mitigation plan |
| Churn Analysis | Churn rate climbed to 8.2% — our top cohort is churning 3x faster than average, and we know why |
| Hiring Status | We're 4 hires behind plan; here's the trade-off we need to make to close the gap before summer |

### Data Slides

| Title (weak) | Headline (strong) |
|---|---|
| Customer Satisfaction Scores | NPS dropped 11 points in Q3 — the lowest since 2021 — and it's concentrated in our enterprise segment |
| Revenue by Segment | SMB revenue is flat; enterprise grew 34% — this tells us where to focus our 2027 growth bet |
| Usage Metrics | 60% of users never return after day 7 — our activation problem is a retention problem in disguise |
| Market Share Data | We gained 2 points of market share this year — but our fastest-growing competitor gained 6 |

### Recommendations / Proposals

| Title (weak) | Headline (strong) |
|---|---|
| Pricing Recommendation | Current pricing leaves $4M on the table annually — here's a two-tier model that captures it without losing volume |
| New Hire Proposal | We need a Head of Partnerships now — every month we wait costs us an estimated $800K in delayed deals |
| Technology Investment | Our legacy stack will require $2M in firefighting next year; a $600K migration now pays for itself in 18 months |
| Go-to-Market Plan | Entering the mid-market segment this quarter puts us ahead of our main competitor's announced expansion |

### Email Subject Lines

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Update | Customer renewal at risk — need your input before Thursday's call |
| Re: Budget | Reallocation request: shifting $200K from paid search to events — here's why |
| Meeting Notes | Key decision from today's meeting: we're pausing Feature X and need your sign-off |
| Question | One question that will change how we scope Q2 — 5-min read |

## Headline Patterns

Use these as starting points when stuck:

**The Gap Pattern**
*"[Metric] is [state] — but [underlying cause] tells the real story"*
Example: *"Conversion is up 18% — but 90% of the gain came from one campaign that ends next month"*

**The Stakes Pattern**
*"[Situation] means we have [timeframe] to [action] before [consequence]"*
Example: *"Our runway gives us 9 months to hit product-market fit before the next raise — here's what we're cutting to get there"*

**The Insight Pattern**
*"[Data] reveals [non-obvious conclusion]"*
Example: *"Our support ticket data reveals that 40% of churn is preventable with a single onboarding fix"*

**The Decision Pattern**
*"We have two options: [option A] or [option B] — here's why [recommended option] is the right call"*
Example: *"We have two options: delay the launch 6 weeks or ship with known issues — here's why the delay is the right call"*

**The Good News / Catch Pattern**
*"[Positive result] — [the caveat that demands attention]"*
Example: *"Our pilot exceeded every metric — but it only works in markets where we have brand awareness, which is 30% of our target"*

## Common Headline Mistakes

**The Non-Claim Headline**: States what is being shown, not what it means.
- Bad: *"Overview of Q3 marketing performance"*
- Good: *"Q3 marketing ROI beat target — but we're over-indexed on one channel that shows signs of saturation"*

**The Vague Positive**: Sounds good but says nothing.
- Bad: *"Strong results across the board"*
- Good: *"Revenue grew 22% YoY — driven entirely by our bottom 20% of customers who we've been underserving"*

**The Data Dump Headline**: Just restates the chart title.
- Bad: *"Chart 3: Monthly active users by cohort"*
- Good: *"New cohorts are activating faster, but 90-day retention is declining — our activation fix may have a leaky bucket problem"*

**The Buried Lead**: The insight is in the last sentence of a paragraph, not the headline.
- Fix: whatever point you were building to, put it first.

## Quick Self-Check

Before finalizing a headline, ask:
1. Does this express a point of view, or just describe a topic?
2. Would a different company or team in a completely different situation be able to use this headline? (If yes, it's too generic.)
3. Does the audience know what to think after reading this, or do they have to infer it from the body?
4. Is there something at stake in this headline?
