# Story Templates for Common Business Scenarios

Use these as starting scaffolds. Customize every bracket — generic details kill the story.

---

## Template 1: The Business Pitch / Proposal

**Best for:** Pitching a new initiative, product, or investment to leadership or clients.

```
SETTING (WHY setup):
[Time marker + situation]. The [market/team/company] has been [context].
Data point that establishes stakes: [X metric showing the opportunity or gap].

CHARACTERS (WHY — human element):
[Customer segment / team / specific person] is currently experiencing [specific pain or opportunity].
For example, [concrete example that makes it feel real].

CONFLICT (WHY climax):
The problem: [obstacle]. This means [consequence if unaddressed].
Every [time unit] we don't act, [cost or missed opportunity].
[Audience] has a choice: [status quo path] or [change path].

BIG IDEA (WHAT):
[Company/team] should [recommended action] because [specific reason tied to conflict],
and if we do, [specific transformation / benefit at stake].

RESOLUTION (HOW):
Our solution: [what it is, in plain terms].
Here's how it works: [3 key points, not feature lists — outcomes].
The ask: [specific decision needed, budget, timeline, approval].
```

**Example headline:** *"Our top-performing customer segment is about to churn — but one targeted intervention could retain 80% of them and add $2M ARR."*

## Template 2: The Executive Status Update

**Best for:** Quarterly business reviews, project health checks, leadership stand-ups.

**Rule:** Lead with the Big Idea. Executives want the "so-what" first.

```
BIG IDEA UP FRONT:
[Project/initiative] is [on track / at risk / ahead of schedule] because [specific reason],
and we need [specific decision/action/awareness] by [date].

SETTING:
As of [date], here is where we stand: [2-3 metrics that matter most].
Context: [what has changed since last update, what external factors matter].

CONFLICT (if applicable):
We are encountering [specific obstacle]. The impact: [consequence in business terms].
This requires [what type of response — decision, resource, escalation].

RESOLUTION:
Our plan: [what we're doing about it, in 2-3 concrete steps].
What we need from you: [clear, single ask].
Next milestone: [specific date + deliverable].
```

**Example headline:** *"Phase 2 is 3 weeks behind because a third-party dependency slipped — we have two options and need a call by Friday."*

## Template 3: The Data Presentation

**Best for:** Presenting research findings, analytics reports, performance data.

**Rule:** Lead with the insight, not the methodology.

```
HEADLINE (WHAT — lead with the "so-what"):
The data shows [key insight]. This means [implication for the audience].

SETTING:
We analyzed [data source / time period / sample]. Here is the baseline: [key metric].

CHARACTERS:
This affects [specific customer / employee / segment].
To make this concrete: [one human example that illustrates the pattern].

CONFLICT:
The gap: [what the data reveals that is surprising, concerning, or significant].
If this trend continues: [projected consequence in 6–12 months].

RESOLUTION:
Based on this, we recommend [specific action].
The expected outcome: [what changes, quantified if possible].
To move forward, we need [ask — approval, budget, team, timeline].
```

**Example headline:** *"Customers who don't complete onboarding in 7 days have a 3x higher churn rate — fixing this single step could improve retention by 18%."*

## Template 4: The Change Initiative / Transformation Story

**Best for:** Rallying a team around a strategic change, culture shift, or new direction.

**Rule:** Position the audience as the hero. The speaker is the guide.

```
SETTING:
[Time marker]. [Industry / company / team] has been operating under [old assumption or approach].
This worked when [context that made it work]. But [what has changed].

CHARACTERS:
Our [customers / team / stakeholders] are now [new situation or expectation].
Specifically, [one concrete example of how the world has shifted for them].

CONFLICT:
If we keep doing what we've always done, [specific negative consequence].
[Competitor / market / customer data] shows the window is [closing / already closing].
This is a burning platform: [what is at stake if we don't act].

BIG IDEA:
We have a choice: [status quo risk] or [bold path forward].
I believe [team/company] is positioned to [specific opportunity] — but only if we [specific change required].

RESOLUTION:
Here's what the new approach looks like: [what changes, in concrete terms].
Here is what stays the same: [what doesn't change — important for trust].
What I'm asking each of you: [specific behavior / decision / commitment].
Here is what success looks like in [time period]: [vivid, specific picture of the transformed state].
```

**Example headline:** *"The way we've managed customer onboarding has served us well for 5 years — but the data shows it's now our #1 churn driver, and we have 90 days to fix it before our biggest competitor makes it a selling point."*

## Template 5: The Email (Short-Form Story)

**Best for:** Any business email where you want action, not just acknowledgment.

```
SUBJECT LINE: [Specific outcome or insight] — [action needed / FYI / decision by date]
Example: "Q4 supplier contract at risk — need your sign-off by Thursday"

BODY:

[CONFLICT — 1-2 sentences]:
[Specific situation that creates urgency or requires a decision].
[Why it matters / consequence if unaddressed].

[CONTEXT — 1-2 sentences if needed]:
[Any background the reader needs to make sense of the conflict].

[BIG IDEA + RESOLUTION — 1-2 sentences]:
[What you recommend / what you've already done / what needs to happen].
[What the outcome will be if they act].

[ASK — 1 sentence]:
[Specific action: "Reply with your approval," "Join the 30-min call Thursday at 2pm," "Review the attached doc and let me know by EOD Friday"].
```

## Quick Story Spine (Pixar-Style)

Use this to quickly sketch a narrative arc when you're stuck:

```
Once upon a time, [setting — the world before the conflict].
Every day, [the normal routine / status quo].
Until one day, [the conflict — what disrupted the status quo].
Because of that, [consequence 1].
Because of that, [consequence 2].
Until finally, [the resolution — how the conflict was overcome].
And ever since then, [the transformation — what is different now].
```

This is a powerful tool for finding your story before you commit to a format. Once you can complete this spine, you have the raw material for any business communication.
